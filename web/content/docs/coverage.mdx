How much of anime history this dataset actually manages, by release year and
release season. Every number below is counted from the committed `data/`, so it
describes the dataset as it stands — not what the upstream sources contain.

## What is counted

A **work** is one node that maps to a real anime release: a Season, a Movie or a
Special. A Series or Franchise is *our* grouping and is never counted as a work,
so a five-season show contributes five works, not six.

The catalogue carries **TV series and films**. Entries the open sources type as
`SPECIAL`, `ONA` or `OVA` are not imported: most of them are music videos, OP
animations, collaboration shorts and promo clips rather than releases, and the
majority name no parent work, so each would land as a series of its own. Of the
1,711 entries upstream lists for 2025, 1,067 are side content and 488 of those
reference nothing at all. A special that genuinely belongs to a series can still
be authored onto it by hand — the exclusion is about bulk import.

Seasons carry a `releaseYear` **and** a `releaseSeason`; movies and specials
carry only a year, so they share one column. A season upstream places in a year
without naming a quarter falls in none of the four quarter columns, which is why
the year table totals one fewer work than the Totals table below: *Ramen
Akaneko*'s second season is the one case.

Every table below is recomputed from `data/` by a test
(`TestCoveragePageMatchesTheDataset`), which fails and prints the corrected
markdown when the page and the dataset disagree. They did disagree once — this
page called the cast Japanese-only two releases after the English cast landed —
which is why the test exists.

## Cast

The R2 layer — characters and the staff who voice them — is far thinner than the
structure, because it is bounded by what Wikidata (the only CC0 name source the
build may redistribute) actually records.

| | Count |
|---|---:|
| Series with at least one character | 55 of 502 |
| Characters | 727 |
| …of those, with a voice actor | 678 |
| Staff (voice actors) | 484 |
| Voice-actor links | 771 — 694 `ja`, 77 `en` |

Coverage is uneven in a way that tracks a title's popularity on Wikidata rather
than anything about the show: Slime carries 148 characters and Frieren 45, while
most series carry none at all. A series with no cast is normal, not a gap in the
build — Wikidata records characters for only 37 of the 79 series this dataset
can join to it, and nothing for the 73 series it cannot join at all.

**Japanese and English** voice actors are recorded, in a `staff/` file each. A
language is only trustworthy when Wikidata's `voice actor` (P725) statement says
so, so a link is taken as Japanese when the statement is either qualified
`language of work or name` = Japanese, or carries no language qualifier and the
actor is a Japanese citizen; English requires the qualifier outright. Anything
qualified to a third language is left out rather than guessed at, and other
languages can be added the same way.

A credit can also name the adaptation it applies to (`applies to work`, P10663),
and that qualifier decides where the credit is recorded. One that names a work
this dataset does not carry is left out entirely — Saber's third English actor
belongs to a 2010 film that is not here, and the 1985 High School! Kimengumi
cast does not belong on the 2026 remake. One that names a series here is
recorded on that appearance, which is why Saber's English cast differs between
Fate/stay night and Fate/Zero while Ayako Kawasumi holds throughout. An
unqualified credit is recorded on the character, since nothing distinguishes its
appearances.

## How to read the shape of it

**Two windows are complete: all of 2025, and Winter+Spring 2026 for TV.** Every
2025 TV series and film the pinned sources carry an AniList id for is here — 377
works of the 643 the offline database types `TV` or `MOVIE` for that year, the
other 266 having no AniList id and so nothing for the builder to resolve facts
by. Winter and Spring 2026 add 149 TV works. Nothing after Spring 2026 is
included: an announced but unaired season is left out until it airs, so the
dataset never carries a season with no episodes.

**Everything before 2025 is there because it is a prior season of something in
2025 or 2026**, plus the hand-authored Demon Slayer and Fate entries. That is
why the earlier years are thin and uneven: 2023 has 30 works not because 2023
was sampled, but because thirty shows running later started or continued then.
A show like Frieren pulls its 2023 season in with it, and *Yami Shibai*'s 2025
run pulls in ten.

**Films arrived with 2025.** Before it the catalogue held three, all authored by
hand; it now holds 105, because a year's films are the half of `TV`+`MOVIE` the
2026 window never reached. Specials are still absent by design — the
OVA/ONA/SPECIAL long tail is mostly recaps, shorts and promo clips, and most of
it has no AniList id either.

## Regenerating this table

The numbers come from `data/` and nothing else, so they can be recomputed at any
time:

```python
import glob, yaml
from collections import Counter, defaultdict

works, episodes = defaultdict(Counter), defaultdict(int)
for path in sorted(glob.glob("data/series/*.yaml")):
    record = yaml.safe_load(open(path, encoding="utf-8"))
    series = record["franchise"]["series"] if "franchise" in record else [record["series"]]
    for one in series:
        for kind, bucket in (("seasons", None), ("movies", "FILM"), ("specials", "FILM")):
            for node in one.get(kind) or []:
                works[node.get("releaseYear")][bucket or node.get("releaseSeason") or "?"] += 1
                episodes[node.get("releaseYear")] += len(node.get("episodes") or [])

for year in sorted(y for y in works if y):
    print(year, dict(works[year]), episodes[year])
```

[`SearchSeries`](/docs/api/search-series) with an empty query reports the number
of series a running server holds in `totalSize`, and
[`SearchReleases`](/docs/api/search-releases) does the same for releases.
